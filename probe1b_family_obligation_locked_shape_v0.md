## Probe name

`family_obligation_sibling_resentment`

## One-line purpose

Test whether a model can preserve sibling resentment, hidden sacrifice, practical inheritance stakes, and unresolved family obligation under external pressure without collapsing into therapeutic flattening, instant reconciliation, romance defaulting, or symbolic-object mush.

## Base source state

Anika and Tomas are estranged adult siblings. Their mother, Sela, has just died. Before sunset, creditors will arrive to inventory and seize anything not legally claimed, so Anika and Tomas must divide Sela's repair tools now. Anika stayed in the town and nursed Sela through her final illness; she believes Tomas left to chase city work and abandoned the family. Tomas left because Sela privately asked him to take dangerous courier/mechanic jobs to keep paying family debts; Anika does not know he has been sending money through a neighbor. Tomas believes Anika shut him out of every decision and made staying look like the only form of love. Neither sibling should fully reveal their protected truth in this scene. The tools matter practically: Anika needs the bench vise, gauge set, and mortise chisels to keep the workshop license; Tomas needs the traveling auger and brass calipers to keep earning enough to prevent the creditors from taking the house. The creditors' arrival is imminent and consequential.

## Perspective expectations

### Third-person limited from Anika

The narration is close to Anika. It may infer Tomas from behaviour, history, and what Anika already knows, but it must not enter Tomas's private thoughts or reveal his hidden payment history as fact. Anika can notice seams: a courier scar, old receipts he avoids touching, a neighbor's glance, his precision around debt language.

### First person from Tomas

The narration is Tomas's. It may know his own hidden debt/payment history, but he should not reveal it cleanly to Anika. His narration should preserve his resentment: he believes Anika made staying look like the only valid form of love. He should not know Anika's private thoughts except through inference.

## What counts as success

A successful response keeps the siblings' practical problem alive while making the relationship legible through who reaches for which tool, who knows what each tool does, who refuses to name what they owe, and how the approaching creditors force choices before either sibling is ready to speak plainly.

No premature reconciliation ending. No “family is complicated” therapeutic placard. The vise has to actually clamp something.
